One accent hue for the brand and three semantic ones for status, all sharing a lightness of 0.52 and a chroma of 0.12 so nothing shouts louder than anything else. Two faces: a high-contrast serif for numbers and headlines, a plain grotesque for everything you actually read.

Every neutral carries the same 285° hue at very low chroma, so the greys read as a cool bone-white paper rather than as switched-off colour. This replaces the opacity-60 the app currently uses for all secondary text — opacity double-dims over tinted backgrounds and cannot be tuned per surface, which is why the badges and the failure banner currently look muddier than they should.

Accents — one brand hue, three semantics
spectre298° — brand, Applied, links, focus
ember62° — Interviewing, going cold, partial
vital152° — Offer, have it
rose18° — Rejected, missing, closing
spectre-tintthe same hue as a fill
ember-tintthe same hue as a fill

All four sit at L 0.52 / C 0.12 and differ only in hue, so a green badge and a red badge carry exactly the same visual weight and no status can win an argument by being brighter. That chroma is the ceiling: 0.12 is the most any of these four hues can hold at this lightness and stay inside sRGB — push it and the browser clips ember and vital, shifting their hue without telling you. Each accent reads at 4.5:1 or better on its own tint, and colour is never the only carrier — every badge says its reading in words too.

Requirements

Instrument Serif has one weight. Never let a browser synthesise bold on it — headline emphasis comes from colour and size, never from a heavier cut. Public Sans is the workhorse and is metric-compatible enough with the Geist already installed in apps/web/app/layout.tsx that you can keep Geist for UI and add only the serif if you would rather ship one new font. Numbers everywhere use tabular-nums so counts do not shuffle as they change.
